Metaphors in Digital Spaces

How do metaphors transform websites into spaces with a sense of place and emotion, and how do these metaphors shape what we notice, understand, or overlook in digital environments?

How do interface metaphors shape users’ expectations, and where do those expectations diverge from how digital systems actually work? My ISP will investigate how language and metaphor shape our understanding and experience of digital spaces. Through readings, case studies, and a series of small web experiments, I will explore how interface metaphors shape users’ expectations and what they reveal or obscure about digital systems. These experiments will inform a final interactive website that brings together my research and design explorations. Rather than treating metaphors as purely visual or linguistic devices, this study approaches them as structures that influence how people perceive and interact with digital systems. Through design experiments, I will investigate the relationship between interface language, technological structures, and user experience.

By the end of this independent study, I will be able to:

Analyze, how spatial metaphors and interface language shape people’s expectations, navigation, and feelings within websites. Question, what these metaphors reveal or hide about digital spaces, including labor, access, and control. Create, build websites to explore how metaphors shape the way content is organized and how people navigate and interact. Observe, use critique and feedback to compare my intended meanings with how others experience the experiments. Express, bring together research and experiments in a final interactive website that explain my design decisions and findings.

Week 1
How Do Metaphors Shape Digital Spaces?
This week will focus on understanding how metaphors influence our perception and experience of digital environments. Through readings, visual research, and case studies, I will investigate how familiar concepts from the physical world are translated into digital interfaces, shaping users' expectations, navigation, and understanding of information.

Week 2
From Metaphor to Interface
This week will explore how metaphors can be translated into different interface structures. Using the same collection of content, I will experiment with metaphors such as a garden, a library, and a room to investigate how each influences the organization, presentation, and experience of information. Initial sketches and Figma prototypes will help identify their possibilities and limitations.

Week 3
Metaphors as Interactive Systems
This week will investigate how metaphors can shape the behavior and interaction of a website beyond its visual appearance. Through technical research and web prototyping, I will explore how navigation, transitions, and interactive elements can reflect the logic of a chosen metaphor. Feedback from critique will inform further development.

Week 4
Building a Metaphorical Digital Space
This week will focus on developing a cohesive digital experience from the previous experiments. I will select and refine a metaphor, examining how its spatial logic can guide navigation, content relationships, and user interaction. The goal is to develop a functional interactive website that brings together my research and design explorations.

Week 5
What Do Metaphors Reveal and Obscure?
In the final week, I will finish my interactive website as a work of hypertext net art and document my research and design process. I will also reflect on how the chosen metaphor changes the way people explore and understand the website, including what it helps them notice and what it might hide.

An independent study project by Jiabao Wu at Rhode Island School of Design (RISD), advised by Kathy Wu during Wintersession 2027.
